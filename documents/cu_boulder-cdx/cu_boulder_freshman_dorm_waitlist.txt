Title: CU Boulder freshman dorm waitlist
Source URL: https://www.reddit.com/r/cuboulder/comments/1t6k8k6/cu_boulder_freshman_dorm_housing/
Source type: Reddit thread
Collected: 2026-06-06
Topic tags: on-campus housing, freshman housing, waitlist, housing capacity

The original poster asked whether CU Boulder admits more freshmen than it has dorm rooms while still requiring freshmen to live on campus. The student concern was that a housing application problem could lead to the waitlist, and the waitlist did not appear to guarantee on-campus housing.

One reply summarized the short answer as yes, then explained that housing was unusually tight because a new dorm had opened, Williams Village was set to expand, and Farrand was under renovation. The commenter estimated about 7,500 freshmen and about 7,800 rooms while Farrand was under construction, but noted that not every room is available to freshmen because some rooms go to second-year students, older students, RAs, and student leaders.

The same reply said the waitlist usually moves over the summer because some admitted students decide not to attend CU Boulder. The commenter advised students to ask about their waitlist position in June, use CU's off-campus housing office if needed, and check CU Boulder email daily because deadlines are enforced.

Other students said they knew incoming freshmen who were waitlisted even after applying soon after the application opened. A response said dorm assignments had not been released yet and that students generally go to a waitlist if they miss the sign-up date. Another student said most people eventually get off the list because there is a lot of movement during the summer.

One longer comment described CU Boulder as broadly under-resourced for its student population. The student said housing is hard to get, the classes students want or need are hard to get, advising can be difficult to access, and facilities such as gyms and libraries are crowded. The comment still noted that people are generally nice and the scenery and nature are strong positives.

Another student wrote that CU "over books" dorms because many freshmen drop out within the first couple weeks, and said the chance of eventually getting a spot was high. Other comments emphasized that students who are waitlisted should keep watching for summer movement rather than assuming they will never get housing.
